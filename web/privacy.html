Privacy Policy

Pocket Ledger · Effective 18 October 2026

In short: your money data stays on your device, unless you turn on sync.
  • No account is needed. Without one, nothing leaves your device.
  • Sync is optional. If you create an account, your data is stored with Google Firebase so it can appear on your other devices.
  • We never sell or share your information, and there are no ads, analytics or tracking.
  • You can delete your account and everything synced to it at any time, in the app or on the web.

This policy explains how Pocket Ledger (“the app”), developed by D. Kezara, handles your information. It covers the Android app, the Windows app and the web version at d-keshara.github.io/pocket-ledger.

Information you enter

Pocket Ledger lets you record things like transactions, amounts, notes, categories, accounts, budgets, savings goals, scheduled payments, your name (used only for the greeting) and your settings.

This is always stored on your own device:

  • Android app: in the app’s private storage on your phone.
  • Windows app: in a file in your user folder (%APPDATA%\PocketLedger).
  • Web version: in your browser’s local storage.

If you don’t create an account, this information is never sent to us or to anyone else.

Optional sync (only if you create an account)

In Settings → Sync & account you can create an account to use the same data on several devices. If you do:

  • Your account: your email address and password are handled by Google Firebase Authentication. The app never stores or sees your password; Firebase keeps only a protected (hashed) form of it. Firebase may also record technical details such as your IP address to protect accounts from abuse.
  • Your money data: everything listed under “Information you enter”, except your light/dark choice and backup reminder, is copied to Google Cloud Firestore and kept in sync with your other signed-in devices. Each change also stores the time it was made and a random code for the device that made it.
  • Where: on Google’s servers in India (region asia-south1, Mumbai). It is sent over an encrypted connection and Google encrypts it when stored.
  • Who can see it: security rules let only your own signed-in account read or change your data. The data is not end-to-end encrypted, so as the developer I could technically view it through Firebase’s admin tools; I don’t look at it, and I never sell or share it.
  • Emails: Firebase sends you emails only when needed for your account, such as confirming your address or resetting your password.

Google acts as the service provider for this storage. See Firebase’s privacy and security information.

Information we collect

Apart from the optional sync described above, none. The app contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools, and it does not collect device identifiers, location, contacts or any other personal data.

Backups and CSV exports

You can choose to save a backup file or export your transactions as a CSV file. These files are created only when you ask for them, and you decide where they go (for example your phone’s storage, Google Drive, or a message to yourself). Once you save or share a file somewhere, that place’s own privacy policy applies to it.

When you restore a backup, the app reads only the file you pick.

Permissions

The Android app asks for internet access, which it uses only for the optional sync. If you never create an account, it sends nothing. When you save a backup or export, Android’s own share menu lets you pick where the file goes.

Web version and hosting

The web version is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you open it, your browser downloads the app’s files from GitHub, which may keep standard technical logs such as IP addresses (see GitHub’s privacy statement). Unless you turn on sync, the app itself sends nothing back, and once loaded it works offline.

Keeping and deleting your data

Synced data is kept for as long as you have an account. When you delete something in the app, it is removed from your account too (a small marker with only its code and the time remains, so your other devices also remove it).

  • Delete your account and everything synced to it: in the app, Settings → Sync & account → Delete account. Without the app, use the account deletion page. Deletion is immediate and can’t be undone.
  • Delete the data on a device: Settings → Erase all data. If you are signed in, this also erases it from your account and your other devices. Uninstalling the Android app, or clearing your browser’s site data for the web version, also removes it from that device.
  • Stop syncing but keep your data: sign out. Your data stays on the device.

Children

Pocket Ledger is a general personal finance tool and is not directed at children under 13, and children under 13 should not create an account.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever starts handling data differently, this page will say so before that version is released.

Contact

Questions about this policy or the app? Open an issue at github.com/d-keshara/pocket-ledger/issues. Please don’t post your email address or money details there.
